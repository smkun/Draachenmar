# Chapter 1: Shining Bargothia Lies in Ruins

## The Fall of a Great Civilization

The Jade and Pearl towers are cracked and broken, crumbling away into dust on the city streets and flotsam in the wrecked harbors of her ancient cities. The great fields of Achenor have been sown with bones and watered with the blood of the fallen people of Bargothia. The Great Vale of Khartun, once renowned for rolling hills painted with Dragon Poppies, Lily Trees, and wildflowers is now grey and ruined, a desolate wasteland. Flowers will no longer grow there, at the site of the final stand of the Bargothian armies. If you stand still and listen, you can almost hear the voices of the men who fell delaying the encroach of the enemy so the dwarves could complete the great gates of the fortress at the Wall… the last bastion against the horrors that had overtaken Old Bargothia.

## The Emissary's Crash and The Wound

Over two hundred and fifty years ago, a fragment splintered from the centennial comet called The Emissary as it passed over the skies of Bargothia and crashed to earth in the Orc Kettles. The Orc Kettles were home to all manner of orc and goblin kind, who were up until that point nothing but a small nuisance to the armies of Old Bargothia, posing only enough of a threat to keep the Shining Lands in a state of uneasy readiness. After the comet hit, the earth shook and the sky burned with fire for three straight nights as the damage from the falling rock soaked into the very earth beneath the Kettles. A great scar was torn in the land: The Wound spread its filth and evil, infecting the soil, water, and air of the Orc Kettles and changing them forever. While many orcs and goblins perished in the blast, the survivors of that issued forth from The Wound were changed. These changed orcs would lead to the downfall of Bargothia and the deaths of untold numbers of good folk.

## The Rise of K'hara

Neither magical scrying nor prayers to the gods have ever revealed what happened in the Orc Kettles the day the stone fell to earth. All that is known is that an orc girl named K'hara, sole survivor of her clan, was transformed on the day now known as Rockfall, and would later rise to power and lead all the orcs of the Kettles against Bargothia. The Bargothians had their own problems to worry about: between the tidal waves, earthquakes, weather changes, and crop failures that followed in the great wracking of the earth, it was years before the leaders of Bargothia took notice of the changes in the Kettles. They finally began to pay attention as waves of strangely mutated creatures issued forth from the Kettles, rampaging through the lands of Bargothia and causing much privation. Swarms of altered deer, vermin, wolves, great cats, and things that no longer matched any description of known species rampaged through what little arable land the Bargothians had left.

## The Ignored Warnings

The orcs were caught up in their own internal chaos as the Kettles boiled with conflict, and orcish refugees fled the Kettles to find sanctuary wherever possible - even with Old Bargothia. But the stories they told did not sway the sympathy of the Bargothian lords, nor did they urge them to action. The warnings of the refugees were not heeded and the terrified orcs with their strange stories of the horrors overtaking the kettles were cast out into the wilderness by the Bargothians, never to be seen again. The Bargothian leadership could not allow them to stay and sow seeds of fear and dissent among the Bargothian people. Refugees continued to seek aid in Bargothia, however, and they all told the same stories of monsters and death and the coming of the K'hara.

## The Stacking - A Warning Ignored

The Hordes of K'hara came forth from the Kettles in a procession of huge wagons crafted from the bones of thunder lizards and drawn by massive pairs of spiked bulletes - a species never previously seen in the known lands. Five thousand orc warriors pulled a massive palanquin amid the procession as it slowly wended its way to the Bargothian borderlands. Well shy of the frontier, the orcs broke out of the formation and assembled into legions of countless thousands and, at the sound of a massive bone horn wrought from the head spike of some massive and ancient sea serpent, the legions proceeded to unload the contents of the wagons at the very edge of the border with Bargothia. The grisly cargo was skulls, over a million orc skulls alone, and hundreds of thousands of hobgoblin and bugbear skulls. There were so many, in fact, that the watching Bargothian border guards had no idea there had ever been that many people in the Kettles.

The unloading and stacking of the skulls took three days. The horde was careful not to encroach on Bargothian territory, and it was said that the leader atop the palanquin never moved in that three-day span. The border guards were soon joined by hundreds of cavalry, aerial legions, and infantry troops ported in by the mages of Bargothia. Magics were woven and wards placed. Sages observed the proceedings worriedly and took careful note of all that transpired in the event they had now begun to call The Stacking. Elves, dwarves, and other fair folk sent delegations to witness the spectacle; even the Dragon Council sent a representative to watch The Stacking transpire.

When the last great wagon was emptied, the Horde regrouped into their squadrons and turned their backs on Bargothia, waiting for their leader's order to return to the Kettles. The great horn sounded twice and strange, winged beasts rose up from the palanquin where they had waited in such stillness that the Bargothians had mistaken them for statues all this time. The beasts dived towards the twelve great pyramids of skulls and proceeded to vomit forth a putrid fountain of amber bile that coursed down over the skulls, filling every gap, hole, and crevasse until the entirety of each colossal pile was fully encased in the sticky liquid. The pyramids smoked as the bile hardened into a resinous ichor, which in the coming days would be discovered by Bargothian scouts to be impenetrable to physical and arcane damage. The Horde did not pay notice to the gathered Bargothian forces before, during, or after The Stacking took place. It was as if, to them, there was no one watching at all. The message, however, was clear to Bargothia: do not cross this line. For the first time, the Bargothians finally understood that something exceptional and terrifying had occurred in the Kettles, and the time had already passed to stop it.

## The Calm Before the Storm

The Kettles went eerily silent after the Horde made their show of force, and in response Bargothia erupted into a frenzy of panic and activity. Couriers traveled to all friendly and neutral nations with word of what had transpired in the borderlands. Alliances were called upon; troops were levied and new militias were raised. Bargothia's economy boomed and crashed in waves as war materials were bought up and stockpiled, the black market flourished, and trading nations increased shipping of all manner of goods and raw materials to the Bargothians. The rumor began to circulate that the nation was hiring, and mercenary companies from far and wide traveled to Bargothia in search of new contracts.

Through all of this, the flow of information from the Orc Kettles slowed to a trickle as the number of refugees fleeing the wasteland dropped, and then stopped entirely. The border guards maintained the policy of interrogating all orcish refugees and, after brief questioning, shipped them all off into the wilderness to join their kin who had come before them. None of the leaders of Bargothia really cared what happened to the refugees; the inhospitable wilds would take them, or they would become some other nation's problem. The orcs would have died in the wilds had it not been for the rangers and druids of the Wildengarde.

## K'hara's Transformation and the Creation of the Pinakla

While Old Bargothia fretted and the Disappearing Vale bustled with divine purpose, the Orc Kettles simmered quietly and slowly. K'hara had risen from a mere orcish girl child to become a coldly merciless and meticulously calculating warlord. A great number of her people had been killed in the wake of The Emissary's landing and the evil that leaked from the wound it had created, but under K'hara's influence, her horde was further culled as it prepared for war. All training was designed for the survival of the fittest, challenges were created for many to enter and only one to leave, and duels were actively encouraged as the Horde fought itself for rank and supremacy. The Horde was like a dangerous beast with a thousand open wounds: though it bled constantly, it fought with the feral ferocity of a cornered animal.

At the top of the Horde were the Pinakla, the most lethal of orcs, who had survived every challenge, been individually recognized by their warlord, and were elevated to service in K'hara's elite legion. The Pinakla trained the lesser troops and still participated in challenges but were no longer allowed to duel. Additionally, they were given K'hara's embrace... a process that endowed each Pinakla with a fraction of the change that had fully consumed their war goddess incarnate. One in ten did not survive, but the remaining nine stepped forth as warriors of such toughness and ferocity that one alone was the equal of ten trained Bargothian knights. All Pinakla were required to go through this painful and dramatic transformation, and at the end of it all, they were not quite orcs anymore. They were something… more.

The Horde transformed thousands of Pinakla in a single year, and K'hara bid them to take as many mates as they wished for the replenishment of the now diminished population of the Kettles. When it was discovered that Pinakla were unable to conceive of children together, they were encouraged to spread out among the orc population of the Kettles and procreate. Orcish folk who had been loyal to K'hara from the beginning but were unfit for warfare had lived in relative comfort under her protective watch. Unaware of the part they were to play in the continuation of the Kettles, they became an integral part of K'hara's initiative to regrow and strengthen the population.

After the Pinakla and orcs were joined, they were instructed by their queen to take great care to ensure the health of both partners and progeny. The conception and birthing of Pinakla children was a dangerous process for the orcs, no matter how careful they were. An orcish woman could bear at most six Pinakla offspring before the hardship killed her. Twins were rare and honored highly, and triplets were especially prized, but no mother survived the birth of such children. As for Pinakla females who took orcish males as mates, it could take more than a hundred couplings just to achieve a single Pinakla pregnancy. Slowly and surely, the population of Pinakla children grew, bringing new life and strength to the Kettles, although maintaining a viable population of Pinakla would be an ongoing worry and weakness of K'hara's Horde through the tides of war.

## The Great War and the Last Stand at Gulanbarak

The Bargothians did not heed the warning and marched its massive army towards the Orc Kettles, a mistake that may have very well brought the destruction of world. Thinking they would take the Orc armies by surprise the armies of Bargothia made up of man, dwarves, elves and dragons made its way to the stack where the first blows were dealt. It did not take long to realize they had made a terrible mistake. Millions of orcs, goblins, giants, and strange creatures never seen met them of the battlefield that day. The Bargothians knew almost immediately they could not win and began their retreat, but the orcs did not stop. For months, the battles raged on and Shining Bargothia began to crumble under this massive horde. It wasn't until what was left of the Bargothian forces made its way to the dwarven mountain city of Gulanbarak, when a blow was dealt to the orcs that finally halted their advance. The Dragon conclave had arrived just in time, dragons of every color, in the hundreds darkened the skies over what is now called the bone fields. It was here where the dragons became extinct, but it was their sacrifice that stopped K'hara and her advancing horde.

Today Gulanbarak makes its home to all the remaining dwarven clans. A massive fortress hundreds of miles long, carved into the very side of the mountain. An oath sworn to protect the north from the Shard Lands to the south, ever vigilant the Dwarves watch to the south…
